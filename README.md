# Stork Wings — Business Model Proposal

A proposal to turn Stork Wings from a shop that owns its stock into a marketplace for
children's clothing that never does.

**[▶ Open the presentation](https://jmendezp19.github.io/StarkWings/)**

Prepared by the **Stork Wings group** for MA50200 Business Operation Management,
San Ignacio University, October 2026.

---

## What this proposes

Stork Wings has a registered trademark, a live website, real inventory and a social
mission. What it does not have, after six years, is a single completed sale. Three
things stopped it: tariffs made imported stock too expensive, payments never settled
into the business account, and the capital that existed was already spent on inventory
that has not moved.

A company that cannot finance inventory cannot run a model that requires buying goods
before selling them. So the proposal is to stop buying and start connecting: families
list the clothes their children have outgrown, the buyer pays first, every item is
checked by hand before it ships on, and the company keeps a share without ever owning
anything.

## Why children only

Narrowing to children is an operations decision, not a marketing one.

- **Children outgrow clothes before they wear them out.** The condition risk that
  threatens any resale model is structurally smaller here.
- **Children's clothes sell in bundles by size.** Two shipping legs cost about the same
  whether the box holds one item or twelve, so the bundle is what makes an inspected
  process affordable at these prices.
- **The seller and the buyer are the same person.** Every few months a size stops fitting
  and the next one is needed, which solves the hardest problem in any marketplace.

## How it gets there

| Phase | What it proves |
|---|---|
| **1 — now** | Sell the stock that already exists. Fix the checkout. Prove the money reaches the account. |
| **2** | Open listings to other families. Same promise, same inspection, someone else's clothes. |
| **3** | A posted price to buy now, or offers from a floor, with the seller deciding whether to accept. |

The promise on the storefront never changes across the three phases: every item checked
by hand. The only thing that changes is who owns the clothes.
