Why it never started

Three walls, and none of them is marketing

1

The merchandise costs too much to import

Most of the supply comes from China, and tariffs made that route expensive. Buying stock abroad stopped being a business.

2

The money cannot leave the platform

Payments are held by the website provider. Even if a customer bought today, the cash would not reach the business account.

3

The capital is already spent and frozen

The stock was paid for out of personal savings and has produced nothing. There is no second round to spend.

Walls one and three are the same wall seen twice: this company cannot finance inventory. Any model that requires buying stock before selling it is already dead here.

What we want to improve

Five fixes, in the order they block each other

1 · The money has to arrive

Nothing else matters while payments sit inside the website provider. A processor that settles into the business account comes first.

2 · One audience, not four

Men and electronics left the storefront. The brand is a stork. It should be allowed to mean children.

3 · Stop owning the stock

Every dollar of inventory is a dollar the company does not have. The seller should own the item until the buyer pays.

4 · Contact that looks like a company

A free mail account on a trademarked domain costs nothing to fix and costs trust every day it stays.

5 · A cause that matches the customer

The buyer is a parent. The giving should be about families, not a separate programme that has nothing to do with the person holding the basket.

The proposal

Stop selling clothes. Start being the place the clothes pass through.

Today the company buys

It pays for stock, stores it, and only then finds out whether anybody wanted it. The money leaves first.

Tomorrow it connects

Families list the clothes their children have outgrown. The buyer pays first. The company keeps a share and never owns a thing.

This is the model our professor named in the interview when we proposed it: just-in-time inventory. Walmart sells shelf space and never buys the product. Kmart and Sears bought the product, and when nobody wanted it they were the ones holding it.

Why children only

Narrowing to children is not marketing. It is what makes the operation work.

Children outgrow clothes before they wear them out

An adult wears a coat until it is ruined. A two-year-old wears one for a season and it is still nearly new. The condition risk that threatens any resale model is structurally smaller here.

Children’s clothes sell in bundles

A parent does not need one shirt. They need the whole next size. A lot of twelve pieces carries the shipping cost that a single eight-dollar shirt never could.

The seller and the buyer are the same person

Every few months a size stops fitting and the next one is needed. That parent supplies the marketplace and shops it, on a cycle, for years.

And the name finally does work. A stork carries babies. Used on a store that also sells electronics, the best asset this company owns means nothing.

Beyond clothing

Baby gear fixes the economics, and forces a second route

A crib is used for two years and a stroller for three. They are expensive, barely worn, and every family eventually has one they no longer need. Six dollars of shipping on a hundred-dollar item is six percent, not seventy-five.

Ships and gets inspected

Clothing, shoes, carriers, baby monitors, books and toys. Light enough for two legs, cheap enough to be worth it in a bundle.

Local pickup only

Cribs, high chairs, strollers, changing tables. Too bulky to ship twice at these rates, so the buyer collects and the platform takes its share without touching the item.

We do not sell

Breast pumps for single users, and car seats. The first is an FDA warning, the second carries a history nobody can inspect.

Why the exclusions are worth more than the sales

The FDA says a single-user breast pump should never be shared or rented, because milk can reach parts of the pump that cannot be cleaned. A car seat that has been in a crash looks identical to one that has not. A brand whose entire promise is “we checked it” cannot sell the two things it is unable to check.

Source: U.S. Food and Drug Administration, consumer guidance on buying and using a breast pump. Crib and car seat safety rules still to be verified against current CPSC standards before any listing opens.

How we get there

Three phases, and the storefront we built is phase one

Phase 1 · now

Sell what is already here

The fifteen pieces she already owns. Fix the checkout, prove the money reaches the account, learn what shipping really costs.

Proves: the plumbing works.

Phase 2

Let other families list

Same photographs, same promise, same inspection. The only change is that the clothes belong to somebody else and the company takes a share.

Proves: supply arrives without capital.

Phase 3

Open the bidding

A posted price to buy now, or offers that climb from a floor, with the seller deciding whether to accept.

Proves: the market can price it.

The promise on the site never changes across the three phases: every item checked by hand. The only thing that changes is who owns the clothes.

The process

One bundle, from a closet to a doorstep

1

A parent lists

Photos, size, condition. A bundle, not a single shirt.

2

A buyer pays

Stork Wings holds the money. Nothing has moved yet.

3

The seller ships in

To the inspection point, with a label the platform provides.

4

We check it

Against the photos and the description. This is the whole promise.

5

We ship out

Repacked, to the buyer, with tracking.

6

The seller is paid

Only after it passes and ships. The company keeps its share.

If it fails

The buyer is refunded. Who pays the return leg is a rule we still have to write.

The flow unit

One bundle. Everything we measure is measured per bundle.

How the price is set

Two ways to buy, and the seller always has the last word

Buy it now

A price the seller posted. The buyer pays it and the bundle is theirs. No waiting, no auction, no uncertainty.

This is what makes day one work, before there is enough traffic for bidding.

Or make an offer

Bidding climbs from a floor the seller sets. When it closes, the seller sees the winning offer and decides whether to take it.

Nobody is ever forced to sell their child’s clothes below what they would accept.

Why the seller accepting matters operationally

The hardest thing to recruit is the first seller. A parent uploading their child’s clothes for the first time is afraid of two things: that it sells for nothing, and that they get cheated. The floor removes the first fear and the final acceptance removes the second. Supply is the scarce resource here, so the rules should favour the supplier.

The inspection point

The whole brand lives in one room

Why it exists

The clothes come from a stranger’s home. Without somebody opening the box, the company is promising a condition it has never seen. One bad bundle on social media costs more than a hundred good ones earn.

Where it starts

In a house. A table, good light, a scale, a printer and a phone camera. The volume at phase one does not justify anything larger, and pretending otherwise burns money the company does not have.

When it has to move

When bundles arrive faster than one person can open, check, repack and ship them. That is the moment the queue starts growing and every order waits longer.

This is the bottleneck, and we should name it before it bites

Nothing in the process can move faster than this room. Advertising harder, listing more bundles or improving the website raises the inflow without raising the outflow, and the only result is a bigger pile and a slower delivery. The number to measure from day one is how many bundles one person can clear in a day.

The giving arm

Under the Wings — support for families, funded by what the marketplace earns

What changed

The current site gives to substance abuse programmes. That is worth doing, but it is not what the person holding the basket came for. We propose widening it to the family as a whole.

What it covers

Counselling for families under strain, parenting support, help for a household in crisis, and awareness so a family asks for help before it breaks.

Why it fits

Every customer is a parent. Every seller is a parent. A programme for families speaks to the same person the business already serves, in the same sentence.

Funded the way the owner described it: a share of the profit on each sale, not of the price. Giving away a percentage of revenue on a five-dollar bundle would cost more than the bundle earns, and a programme that bankrupts the business helps nobody.

“Under the Wings” is our proposed name for the programme and is not yet decided. The percentage is still open.

The same $6 either way

Two shipping legs cost roughly the same whether the box holds one shirt or twelve. Only the sale price changes.

So bundles are not a preference

They are the condition under which an inspected, two-leg process can exist at all at these prices.

And single items ship direct

Below a price we still have to set, the item goes straight from seller to buyer with no inspection, the way eBay routes only its expensive categories.

The $3.00 per leg is the owner’s own USPS rate for a five-pound ground parcel, given in the September 30 interview. The $8 item and the $40 bundle are illustrative figures chosen to show the effect, not observed sales.

What it takes to start

Six steps to the first completed sale in six years

1 · A payment processor that pays out

Settling into the business account, tested with one real transaction before anything else is announced.

2 · The fifteen pieces listed properly

Grouped into bundles by size, photographed the way the new storefront already does it.

3 · A written condition standard

What passes and what does not, on one page, so the check is the same every time and by anyone.

4 · The return rule, decided

Who pays when a bundle fails inspection. Today the site says no returns at all, which will not survive the first complaint.

5 · A company email address

On the domain the company already owns. Costs almost nothing and is the cheapest trust the brand can buy.

6 · One sale, start to finish

Listed, paid, shipped, received, money in the account. Everything else is a plan until this happens once.

None of these six requires buying inventory, hiring anyone or raising money.

What we need decided

Four questions only the owner can answer

1

Children only, or keep selling to women as well? Everything we modelled assumes children first.

2

What does the company keep on each sale, and is it a percentage, a flat fee, or a percentage with a floor and a ceiling?

3

Above what price does a bundle go through inspection, and below what price does it ship straight from the seller?

4

Where is the inspection room, and who opens the boxes when there are five a day instead of one?

The brand, the website and the mission have been ready since 2019. What has been missing is a process. That is the part we can build without money.
